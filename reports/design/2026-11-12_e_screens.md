## In short
1. **Both candidate rules are stopped, and nothing goes on to 20 seeds.** The Scots pine rule (arm A) makes the forest much more even: the spread of heights at 45 falls from 1.06 m to 0.51 m, where a measured stand has about 3 m. Birch's own rule (BIRCH) keeps that spread where it is (1.09 m), but it narrows the range of heights at 50 and 60 and takes a quarter to a third off the spread of diameters.
2. **Top height did not fall. It rose in every arm,** by 0.3-0.9 m at 50 and 60, so the owner's rule holds. The pine rule lands on the site curve (21.94 / 23.70 m against 21.95 / 23.75). The birch rule reads 0.32 / 0.15 m above it, and about half of its rise is the way top height is read, not taller trees.
3. **Height is no longer drawn, but under the two candidate rules it is not earned either.** The random draw's share of height falls from 0.85 / 0.96 at 50 / 60 to 0.00-0.18. The year a tree came up takes its place (0.54-0.68). Under the birch rule, trees that came up one to three years late are 56 % of the stand at 15 and 8 % at 60 (control: 58 % at 15 and 53 % at 60).
4. **Most of what changed comes from taking the draw off height, not from either rule.** A third fewer stems at 30, the lost diameter spread and the lower leaf area are all in the run with no rule at all (T2's arm N). Light at 60 is over its 19.8 % limit on these seeds in three arms of four, by about 1 standard error at most. With each arm's paired shift added to the 20-seed control it is over in all four at 60, A by 0.01 (19.81, with no rounding). It is the likely fail on 20 seeds (SUSPECTED).
5. **The forecasts written before the runs** were right that A would fail, and right on stem numbers for A, BIRCH and D-BIRCH. They were wrong on top height (higher in every arm), on the birch rule's height spread (0.9-1.1 m, not 1.3-2.4 m) and on diameter spread (a rise was forecast for BIRCH only, and it fell; the other three arms fell below their forecast ranges). The information arm, A-early, finished and is included.
