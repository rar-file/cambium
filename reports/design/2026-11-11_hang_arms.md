## 1. The decision rule, written before any run

**Which measures count.** The photos can carry only what `2026-11-11_hang_measures.md` section 6 says they can:
- **Counted (primary):** the near-vertical share (H5) read in each solitary photo's own window, open-grown model, against Oslo 0.31, Meilen 0.23 and Hallinden 0.32. Ages 30, 50 and 60: the photos are of old trees and say nothing firm about 20 or younger.
- **Counted only as a gate on the primary:** the four-part H5 test (`closer_on_h5`): nearer the photos, H1 (share of branch length that hangs) falls, upright wood pointing down falls, upright wood pointing up does not rise. A setting that turned hanging twigs upright would pass H5 alone and fail this.
- **Side, reported, used only to break ties:** the stand model in the windows of the group and edge photos (Tuntorp 0.31, Brastad 0.26, Russia 0.35), a weaker comparison (one model tree against overlapping real trees); crown width over height in the solitary windows (photos 0.46-0.55; the model is narrower).
- **Not counted:** H1-H4 and H6, which have no real counterpart yet (only by-eye figures for H3); tip direction, traced run and the fringe, which read too weakly. All are reported.

**When an arm is better than today's** (`tools/hang_arms.py`, `toward` and `judge`; seeds 1-5 of both, paired by seed):
1. In each of the three solitary windows at 50 and at 60 (six cells), the arm's near-vertical share moved toward that photo's reading by more than twice the standard error of the seed-by-seed change, and did not pass it: its mean is not on the far side of the photo by more than twice its own standard error. At 30 it is not worse by more than twice the standard error.
2. The four-part H5 test holds in all six cells, on the seed means.
3. Guards, on the stand trees at 50 and at 60, means over seeds: leaf area index inside 3.6-5.4 (Hagemeier & Leuschner 2019) and light under the canopy at most 19.8 % (Hagemeier & Leuschner 2019b). Not loosened.

**Ranking.** Arms that pass come first, then the rest. Within each group, by the remaining gap: seed by seed, the mean over the six cells of how far the arm reads from each photo; smaller is better. Two neighbouring arms count as separated only if the paired difference of that gap exceeds twice its standard error. Arms the numbers cannot separate are ordered by the side measures (stand windows, then crown width), and only then by looking at their leafless pictures beside the photos, on the PC. Nothing is chosen by taste.

**Today's arm.** Open-grown: the baseline's own trees, `node_hang_open3` seeds 1-5 (the growth code is unchanged since). Stand-grown: grown again, because the baseline kept no light; its hang numbers must come out identical to `node_hang_stand3` seeds 1-5.

**What `twig_end_buds` 2 does** (CONFIRMED in the code and the species file at this commit; option J of the audit). `species/betula_pendula.yaml:1840-1842` holds it at 1, "off by default". It is read every year in the bud split (`treegen/growth.py:1013-1020`), only while `crown_frame.earned` is on (it is, by default): on every axis other than the stem (limbs, forks and twigs of any thickness, hanging or not), a lateral bud may start a long shoot only if it sits within `twig_end_buds` nodes of its own axis's newest node; every other lateral bud is a short shoot. At 1 only the newest node's lateral bud qualifies, so a twig mostly just lengthens; at 2 the next node back qualifies as well, so an axis can end in a fork each year. Nothing else changes. Each qualifying bud's share still goes with its own light and its limb's vigour, and with gravimorphism twice: in the split, times max(0.05, 1 + g x f(r) x the bud's upward direction), where f(r) is near 0 on twigs much finer than the softness radius (`growth.py:993`); and in the earned frame's share, times max(0.05, 1 + g x the upward direction of the shoot it sits on), with g = 1 and no f(r) (`growth.py:1802-1803`, "a bud on a shoot that hangs doesn't start a long shoot"). So on a twig 30 degrees below level a lateral bud keeps half its share, at 60 degrees 13 %, and on a strand hanging straight down only the 5 % floor. It becomes a long shoot only past the long-shoot threshold (5 nodes), and a shoot heading down is capped at the hanging share of a level shoot's length (`growth.py:2546-2549`). So it does reach the twigs' own hanging sprays, but on near-vertical strands through the 5 % floor only; SUSPECTED before the runs that it will branch level and gently drooping twigs far more than the cords. It is reachable with `--set crown_frame.twig_end_buds=2`: `checkpoint.set_values` sets it on the loaded species (checked on the PC: 1 before, 2 after, 0.15 s). No code changes. Not sourced: no paper gives a birch twig's count of end buds that start long shoots; H4 has no real counterpart yet (section 1).
