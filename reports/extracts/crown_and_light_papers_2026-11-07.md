# Three papers read from mdpi.com, 2026-11-07

Read in the maintainer's browser at the maintainer's request, from the publisher. All are open access under CC BY. These are my notes of the numbers, not copies. Figure readings are by eye and marked so.

## Hagemeier & Leuschner 2019a, Forests 10:265: crown architecture

Adult trees 22-26 m tall in closed stands, northwest and central Germany; 6 birch felled and measured by 2 m layer.

| Measure, *Betula pendula* | Value |
|---|---|
| Leaf area by height | Entirely in the upper half of the stem; most at about 0.80 of tree height. The crown's radius by height follows the same pattern |
| Leaf angle to the horizontal | Crown average 49 deg; about 55 deg at the top; typically 30 deg or more below 0.5 of height, about 30 deg in the lowest shade crown. Most leaves hang downward |
| Leaf area density, m2/m3 | y = 0.142 + 2.91 x^9.02, x = relative height (Figure 6's fit): about 0.3 low, about 3 at the top |
| Leaf size | Grows linearly from the top down; smallest at the top |
| LAI, litter buckets, two stands | 3.6 and 5.4 |

**Why it matters.** In closed stands the widest crown is high up, at about 0.8 of height. A cone that's widest low down is wrong for a forest tree, and a long bare lower stem is right. These are stands: trees in the open differ.

## Hagemeier & Leuschner 2019b, Forests 10:925: light

| Measure, *Betula pendula* | Value |
|---|---|
| Stand 1 | Age 45, basal area 25.2 m2/ha, 880 stems/ha, DBH 19 +- 6 cm, height 20 +- 3 m |
| Stand 2 | Age 67, basal area 28.5 m2/ha, 420 stems/ha, DBH 25 +- 3 cm, height 25 +- 3 m |
| Light on the forest floor | Median 14.8 %, individual values 6.4-19.8 %; overcast, around noon; stands 1 and 2 |
| LAI (Table 5) | 4.6, mean of the two stands |
| KPAR | 0.37, diffuse light |
| Clumping | 0.42 in Table 5, 0.41 in the text |
| Leaf area density | Sun crown 3.2, shade crown 0.3 m2/m3 |
| Lowest shade leaves' light | 21.0 %: a median of about 136 readings at the darkest places shade leaves were found (site 11) |
| Saplings 50-90 cm | 21.0 % in the text; about 20 % in Figure 6 (site 11) |
| Figure 6, shade leaves, by eye | Box about 19.7-21.7 %, whiskers about 18.7-26.7 % |

**Q19 answered.** The 21.0 % is a median, not a floor: half of those darkest-placed leaves sit below it, down to about 18.7 % by eye. So a hard death line at 0.21 is too high. Test A's 0.18 lies just under the lowest reading, which gives that tuned value some support, though it's still tuned. The methods call minimum light demand the lowest reading, while the results give medians, so the paper isn't clean on this.

**Note on the light band.** 6.4-19.8 % is the spread of single readings. The middle of it is 14.8 %, and stands sit at LAI 4.6. Test A at 18.8 and 19.4 % with LAI 4.27 and 4.09 passes, but sits at the thin, bright edge. The target isn't changed by this.

## Zeltins et al. 2018, Forests 9:52: limbs at wide spacing

Grafted clones of plus-trees, age 40, 400 trees/ha on a 5 x 5 m grid, central Latvia, unmanaged after early cleaning. Preselected for good form, so not ordinary birch.

| Trait | Mean | Range | SD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branch angle, deg | 43.2 | 15.0-80.0 | 10.4 |
| Height, m | 26.2 | 15.3-31.6 | 2.2 |
| DBH, cm | 27.7 | 14.2-45.8 | 5.6 |
| Lowest living branch, m | 11.2 | 1.8-18.0 | 2.7 |
| Mean projection of crown, m | 2.9 | 1.1-6.3 | 0.8 |
| Double tops, % of trees | 34.9 | 6.0-75.1 | |

**Limits.** The methods don't define branch angle or projection of crown. Angle from vertical at the stem is my assumption; a radius for the projection is too. The species file's "43" is this mean.
