## 3. Top height on the curve: OK

`naslund_top_height` (`tools/scorecard.py:203-228`) does what p. 58 and row 44 say:
- It fits h = 1.3 + d^2/(a + b d)^2 to the plot's live trees.
- It reads the curve at the mean diameter of the 100 thickest per hectare, with a fractional last tree.
- It shows those trees' mean height beside the result.

Elfving names no curve for this definition: the only tree-height functions are Soderberg's (Appendix 8, p. 57). My own recalculation gave the same figures as `top_height_check.txt`, for example plot_1 at 60: 30.97 and 29.56 m. The fewer-than-10-trees note prints.

**The curve overshoots the thick trees it's read for:**

| Age | Curve (scored) | Those trees' mean height | Tallest tree on the plot | Band top |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 15.43 | 15.38 | 16.28 | 13.61 |
| 30 | 21.78 | 21.47 | 22.01 | 19.24 |
| 40 | 26.17 | 25.43 | 26.06 | 22.79 |
| 50 | 28.88 | 27.92 | 28.82 | 25.12 |
| 60 | 30.59 | 29.41 | 30.32 | 26.73 |

All values are means of the 5 plots, in metres. At 50 and 60, the curve reads above the plot's tallest tree in 7 of 10 cases.

- **No verdict depends on it:** both measures FAIL at every age.
- **M1's summary leans on it:** "nearly 4 m too tall" at 50 and 60 is 3.76 and 3.86 m on the curve, but 2.80 and 2.68 m on the trees' own mean height.
- **Nobody approved the fit method:** an unweighted straight line through all live trees isn't in the maintainer's answer (question A4).
- **The birch data were different:** p. 58 says the birch function rests on "266 felled and sectioned top height trees". Eriksson et al. remain unopened.
- **Small points:** the model's DBH is under bark (`targets.yaml:168`), while Soderberg's d on p. 57 includes bark; the effect is not calculated. The docstring at `tools/top_height_check.py:5` is out of date.
